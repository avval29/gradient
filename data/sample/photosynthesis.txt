Photosynthesis is the process by which green plants prepare their own food using sunlight, water and carbon dioxide. It takes place mainly in the leaves, inside tiny structures called chloroplasts. Chloroplasts contain a green pigment called chlorophyll, which absorbs energy from sunlight. Water is absorbed by the roots from the soil and carried to the leaves through the xylem. Carbon dioxide enters the leaves through small pores called stomata. Using the energy of sunlight, the plant converts carbon dioxide and water into glucose and releases oxygen as a by-product. The glucose is used for energy or stored as starch. Photosynthesis is important because it provides food for almost all living organisms and keeps the oxygen level of the atmosphere balanced.
