The water cycle describes how water moves between the Earth's surface and the atmosphere. Heat from the Sun causes water from oceans, lakes and rivers to evaporate and rise as water vapour. Plants also release water vapour through their leaves, a process called transpiration. As the vapour rises it cools and condenses into tiny droplets that form clouds. When the droplets grow heavy enough they fall as precipitation, such as rain, hail or snow. Some of this water flows over the land into rivers and lakes, and some soaks into the ground and becomes groundwater. The water eventually returns to the oceans, and the cycle begins again. The water cycle supplies fresh water to living things and helps to spread heat around the planet.
